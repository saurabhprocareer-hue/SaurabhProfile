Executive summary

The US health insurance industry is in its worst profitability crisis in a generation. Industry-wide profit margins fell from 3.8% in 2020 to 0.4% in 2025, and the combined ratio now exceeds 100% — insurers lose money on every underwriting dollar before overhead. Net income fell from $31 billion to $6 billion in five years, driven by rising hospital costs, surging use of specialty drugs (especially GLP-1s) and high-acuity procedures including oncology, joint replacement and advanced cell therapies.

This paper proposes a structured Medical Value Travel (MVT) programme that directs eligible members to premier, JCI-accredited Indian hospital networks for elective and semi-elective high-cost procedures. With savings of 65–90% per procedure versus US list prices, even modest adoption by 5–15% of eligible members produces transformative results.

Section 01

The US healthcare cost crisis

The United States spends more on healthcare per capita than any other nation, by a wide margin, yet lags peer nations on life expectancy, chronic disease burden and timely access. The causes are well documented: an opaque, fragmented delivery system, little price transparency, administrative duplication, hospital consolidation and pharmaceutical pricing unconstrained by negotiation.

Rising medical costs: the core driver

Hospital and medical expenses paid by US insurers rose by $85 billion (8.9%) in a single year (2023–2024), driven by record utilisation, clinical labour inflation and growth in high-cost interventions.

The GLP-1 surge: a new structural cost

GLP-1 receptor agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound, Mounjaro) are adding a permanent layer of pharmaceutical spend. Weight-loss GLP-1 fills more than doubled in 2024, and 12.4% of US adults reported using one as of Fall 2025. At $1,027–$1,349 per month, annual cost per patient is $12,300–$16,200. Use among Americans without diabetes rose 643% between 2017 and 2022.

Pharmaceutical cost arbitrage. US semaglutide costs roughly five times the price in the next most expensive country. Generic semaglutide is legally made and sold in India for about $50–$100 a month — a potential saving of $10,000–$15,000 per patient per year.

Cancer: the largest catastrophic claims category

Over 2 million Americans are expected to be diagnosed with cancer each year. The National Cancer Institute reports an average initial cost of $43,516 per patient, and last-year-of-life costs averaging $109,727. Chemotherapy ranges from $27,000 for a standard 6-month course to over $200,000 for advanced targeted therapy; CAR-T lists at $400,000–$727,000 per course.

Section 03

India as a medical tourism destination

India is not simply a low-cost alternative — it is an accredited, technology-rich ecosystem able to deliver complex surgical and oncology care at a fraction of US prices. Over 7.3 million medical tourists visited India in 2024, and the government's Heal in India initiative has invested in infrastructure, international patient services, digital coordination and medical visas. The market was valued at $8.7 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $16.2 billion by 2030.

Incentive economics. The full package — business-class flight $4,000, companion economy $1,200, 15-day hotel $1,500, companion meals $750, cash reward $3,000, coordinator $500, follow-up $500 — totals about $11,450 per case. Even so, the net saving on a knee replacement remains about $25,050 (vs $45,000 US cost), and on CAR-T it exceeds $380,000 per case.

Section 08

Quality assurance: accredited partners

Clinical quality is the paramount concern. Network participation is restricted to hospitals holding JCI (Joint Commission International) accreditation — the international equivalent of the Joint Commission's US hospital standard. India has 30+ JCI-accredited hospitals, concentrated in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad and Bangalore.

Apollo HospitalsJCI · NABH · NABL · ISO 9001. India's largest private network (73+ hospitals), founded 1983; patients from 150+ countries. Da Vinci Xi robotics, CyberKnife, Southeast Asia's first proton therapy, CAR-T and BMT programmes.
Max HealthcareJCI · NABH. Max Saket is one of North India's most advanced oncology and orthopaedic programmes. Da Vinci cancer surgery, Novalis Tx radiation, HIPEC; FICCI Healthcare Delivery Award winner.
Fortis HealthcareJCI · NABH · ISO 9001. Fortis Memorial Research Institute (Gurgaon) ranks among India's top multi-specialty hospitals; 1,000+ open-heart surgeries a year, BMT centre, dedicated international patient department.
Medanta — The MedicityJCI · NABH · NABL. Founded by Dr. Naresh Trehan; 1,250-bed quaternary care hospital in Gurgaon. Among Asia's highest-volume cardiac programmes; robotics across 7 specialties; liver and kidney transplant.

Section 09

Implementation roadmap

Months 1–3
Programme design & partner selectionRFPs to Apollo, Max, Fortis and Medanta; site visits and clinical audits; master service agreements with volume tiers; legal framework for consent, liability and HIPAA record sharing; travel management partner for block airfare and hotels.
Months 3–6
Platform & care coordinationA digital Medical Value Travel platform: eligibility portal, procedure calculator, telemedicine scheduling, coordinator assignment, real-time case tracking and post-return follow-up, integrated with claims. Train a 5–8 FTE coordination team for a 1M-member plan.
Months 4–8
Pilot launch (orthopaedics)Soft launch with knee and hip replacement at two partners (Apollo Chennai, Max Delhi). Target 50–100 cases; gather cost, outcome and satisfaction data; refine processes and member materials.
Months 8–14
Full programme launchExpand to oncology, spinal and cardiac; add Fortis and Medanta. Launch member communications via brokers, employer wellness programmes and digital channels. Begin the GLP-1 pilot for members travelling for surgery.
Year 2+
Scale & optimisationRenegotiate volume discounts on actual case flow; expand to self-funded employers; launch employer-matching incentives; pursue a regulatory importation channel. Target 10–15% adoption of the eligible pool.
